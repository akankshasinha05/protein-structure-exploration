1. THE BIOPHYSICAL THEORY (IN EASY LANGUAGE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is a Protein?
Proteins are biological nano-machines that perform almost all functions in living systems:
speeding up chemical reactions (enzymes), carrying oxygen (hemoglobin), protecting against
pathogens (antibodies), and providing structural scaffolding (collagen).

A protein's function is determined entirely by its 3D Shape!
Protein structure is organized into 4 hierarchical levels:

1. Primary Structure:
   - The linear string of amino acids linked together by covalent peptide bonds (-CO-NH-).
   - There are 20 standard amino acids (e.g. Alanine, Glycine, Cysteine, Valine).

2. Secondary Structure:
   - Regular, local folding of the polypeptide backbone held together by hydrogen bonds
     between backbone C=O and N-H groups.
   - Alpha Helix (α): A tight, right-handed coiled spiral with 3.6 amino acid residues per turn.
     Hydrogen bonds connect residue i to residue i+4.
   - Beta Sheet (β): Extended strands of polypeptide chains lying side-by-side (parallel or
     antiparallel), forming pleated sheets stabilized by hydrogen bonds between adjacent strands.
   - Random Coils / Loops: Flexible, non-repetitive segments connecting helices and sheets.

3. Tertiary Structure:
   - The full, 3-dimensional spatial folding of a single polypeptide chain.
   - Driven by hydrophobic collapse (greasy water-fearing amino acids cluster inside the core,
     while water-loving hydrophilic residues face outside).
   - Stabilized by salt bridges, hydrogen bonds, and covalent Disulfide Bridges (S-S bonds
     formed between pairs of Cysteine residues).

4. Quaternary Structure:
   - When multiple folded polypeptide chains (called "subunits") assemble together into a functional
     multimeric protein complex (e.g. Hemoglobin is an α2β2 tetramer made of 4 subunits).

The 3 Model Proteins Analyzed in this Project:
1. Crambin (PDB: 1CRN):
   - A small seed plant protein (46 residues). Famous because it has an ultra-high resolution
     structure with 3 stabilizing disulfide crosslinks.
2. Myoglobin (PDB: 1MBN):
   - The oxygen storage protein in diving mammal muscle (153 residues). Has a classic "globin fold"
     composed entirely of 8 alpha helices and 0 beta sheets, holding an iron-heme prosthetic group.
3. Hemoglobin (PDB: 1HHO):
   - The oxygen transporter in human red blood cells. A 4-subunit heterotetramer (287 residues)
     holding 4 oxygen-binding heme cofactors, displaying allosteric cooperativity.

3. LINE-BY-LINE / BLOCK-BY-BLOCK CODE EXPLANATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. explore_structure.py:

Lines 5-10: AA_3TO1 Dictionary
-> A mapping converting 3-letter biochemical amino acid codes (ALA, CYS, GLU, MET, etc.)
   into standard single-letter codes ('A', 'C', 'E', 'M').

Lines 12-63: parse_pdb_file(filepath)
-> Opens raw Protein Data Bank (.pdb) files and parses standard crystallographic record headers:
   - 'HEADER': Date, classification, and PDB ID.
   - 'TITLE': Official scientific name of the macromolecule.
   - 'SEQRES': Primary amino acid sequence for each protein chain.
   - 'HELIX': Start and end residue numbers of each recognized alpha helix.
   - 'SHEET': Start and end residue numbers of beta sheet strands.
   - 'SSBOND': Covalent disulfide bonds between Cysteine sulfur atoms.
   - 'HETATM': Non-protein ligands and prosthetic groups (e.g. HEM = heme iron group, PO4 = phosphate).
   - 'ATOM': Counts total 3D atomic coordinates.
-> Returns a clean, structured Python dictionary of the entire macromolecular architecture.

Lines 65-70: get_amino_acid_counts(res_list)
-> Tallies frequencies of individual amino acid residues across a chain.

Lines 72-106: plot_secondary_structures(results, output_path)
-> Computes the exact percentage of residues in:
   1. Alpha Helices (cornflower blue)
   2. Beta Sheets (salmon orange)
   3. Loops / Coils (light gray)
-> Renders a stacked bar chart comparing all 3 proteins side-by-side.
-> Saves the 300 DPI graphic to 'figures/secondary_structure_composition.png'.

Lines 108-156: main()
-> Discovers all .pdb files in 'data/'.
-> Iterates through each protein, parses the coordinates, and prints an elegant summary:
   * Title, chains, total residues
   * Number of helices and residue coverage
   * Number of beta sheets and residue coverage
   * Disulfide bridges and bound ligands
   * First 40 residues of each chain's single-letter sequence
-> Calls plot_secondary_structures() to output the comparative chart.

---

B. generate_diagrams.py:

Lines 4-50: generate_structure_levels_diagram(output_path)
-> Draws a 4-panel educational schematic:
   - Panel 1: Primary structure (beads on a string representing peptide bonds).
   - Panel 2: Secondary structure (sine-wave alpha helix and directional beta arrows).
   - Panel 3: Tertiary structure (3D folded loop with a yellow dashed disulfide bond).
   - Panel 4: Quaternary structure (four interlocking colored subunit domains).
-> Saves to 'figures/protein_structural_levels.png'.

Lines 52-95: generate_alpha_beta_diagram(output_path)
-> Draws a side-by-side comparison illustrating the spiral helical pitch of an alpha helix
   versus the pleated parallel/antiparallel hydrogen bonded strands of a beta pleated sheet.
-> Saves to 'figures/alpha_helix_vs_beta_sheet.png'.

---

C. PyMOL Scripts (scripts/*.pml):
- 'load data/1mbn.pdb': loads the 3D atomic coordinate geometry into PyMOL.
- 'hide everything': hides messy default wireframe sticks.
- 'show cartoon': renders smooth secondary structure ribbons.
- 'color marine, ss h': colors alpha helices blue.
- 'select heme, resn HEM': finds and selects the heme prosthetic group.
- 'show sticks, heme': shows the catalytic heme molecule in stick representation.
- 'ray 1600, 1200': performs high-end ray tracing for light reflection and shadows.
